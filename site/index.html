Post-quantum STARK verifier for QRL 2.0

QRL 2.0 has no pairing precompiles, so pairing-based SNARK verifiers cannot run there. Validity proofs on QRL take the shape of a STARK: hashing and field arithmetic, post-quantum by construction. QuantaStark verifies Plonky3-compatible proofs on the 512-bit QRVM in Hyperion, measured on a live node, together with the prover, a fact registry and a bridge skeleton for the L1 side of a rollup.

Block cap

40 of the 44 measured cells sit at or below the 8,000,000 gas target. The largest, fib_c1-binary_n20 with a 388 KB proof, takes 17,065,391 gas, 85 percent of the 20,000,000 consensus block cap.

Transaction-pool limit

The execution client refuses transactions above 131,072 bytes, so proofs above about 123 KB need a raised pool cap or staged verification before they can be submitted as one transaction. Calldata is 20 to 43 percent of a verification.

Release gate

A full execution, beacon and validator composition on chain 3151909 deployed every preset and verified fib_c3_n20 end to end. The gas is deterministic QRVM gas, so the cells carry over to the public network unchanged.

How it works

Hash-based from the prover to the bridge

The proof system is Plonky3 uni-stark over Goldilocks with the quadratic extension and two-adic FRI. Every choice below was measured against the QRVM's gas table before it went into the verifier.

01

Prover pinned to Plonky3 0.7.0-rc.1

A Rust workspace produces the proofs, the test vectors and their mutation sets. The crate version is pinned in prover/PLONKY3_VERSION and recorded in every compile manifest, so a proof and the verifier that checks it always name the same upstream release.

02

keccak256 Fiat-Shamir and Merkle

Every hash in the proof system goes through the KECCAK256 opcode: 30 + 6 gas per 64-byte word, against 388 gas per call of the SHAKE256 precompile. A proof verifies a few thousand hashes, so the cheapest hash decides the design.

03

Goldilocks with modexp inversion

p = 2^64 - 2^32 + 1 needs the fewest extension-field operations of the common STARK fields. Hot loops run in Yul on native 512-bit words, and field inversion goes through the 0x05 modexp precompile.

04

Pruned multi-opening proofs

Query openings share one pruned Merkle multi-proof per commitment and FRI folds with arity 8, the cheapest schedule in both bytes and gas. Proofs travel as packed bytes calldata: 8-byte little-endian elements, 32-byte digests, exact length enforced before any query data is read.

05

Fact registry keyed by public values

StarkFactRegistry stores keccak256(verifier, programId, keccak256(publicValues)). Proof bytes carry unobserved witness bytes and are malleable, so a proof hash never keys anything; the bridge looks up the fact.

What the bridge does

Three flows, all Hyperion, high level, no assembly. Gas is the receipt on the developer node with a four-leaf withdrawal tree.

  1. Deposits.

    deposit() hashes the sender, the amount and the deposit index into a leaf, folds it into an append-only accumulator and emits Deposited. An L2 batch later proves that it consumed the deposits in order.

  2. Batches proven by STARK.

    submitBatch(prevRoot, newRoot, publicValues, proof) requires prevRoot to equal the current state root and the public values to encode both roots as sixteen Goldilocks limbs. The registry verifies the proof, or finds the fact already registered, and the state root advances. 155,618 gas plus the verifier's own gas.

  3. Withdrawals authenticated by ML-DSA-87.

    withdraw proves a leaf under the state root and checks an ML-DSA-87 signature through precompile 0x03 over a SHAKE256 digest (precompile 0x06) of the leaf and the recipient, with the context QP-WITHDRAW-v1. The leaf is marked spent before the transfer. 345,791 gas, 125,000 of them in the precompile.

The skeleton has no escape hatch, challenge window, forced inclusion, sequencer role, pausing or upgradeability yet. docs/BRIDGE.md lists every simplification, and the architecture document gives the order in which they get added.

Research status and roadmap

Measured, decided, unaudited

QuantaStark is a DigitalGuards research project: experimental, unaudited, and subject to breaking changes. It exists to measure what post-quantum validity proofs cost on QRL 2.0 and to inform an L2 design. Nothing in the repository is production software, and nothing in it should secure funds.

  • Verifier, prover, vectors, registry and bridge skeleton complete and measured on the developer node (milestones M0 to M8). Every valid vector of the sixteen presets verifies; every mutated vector reverts with its recorded error.
  • Release gate run on the Kurtosis composition (chain 3151909): all presets deployed, fib_c3_n20 verified end to end, gas report regenerated.
  • L2 architecture decision (milestone M9) derived from the measured numbers, with a decision table and the open questions.
  • No external review yet. The presets carry 116 to 118 conjectured bits of soundness under a conjecture that is under active revision; an audit of the verifier and the circuits is part of the plan.
  • Public deployment waits for the QRL 2.0 public testnet relaunch. Until then the bridge above stays a preview.

The L2 recommendation

Build a validity rollup whose data availability lives in QRL calldata and whose final on-chain proof is the c3 STARK that StarkVerifier already checks. Aggregate batch proofs with two-layer recursion, Poseidon2 inside and keccak256 at the last layer, and let L2 accounts sign with a Poseidon2 hash-based scheme. Keep ML-DSA-87 at the L1 boundary, through the bridge.

Next steps, in order

  1. M10 Poseidon2 AIR and layout version 2 Multi-commitment layout with quotient chunks, measured on the developer node; a Kurtosis gas report for the existing cells.
  2. M11 Data binding in the bridge A keccak AIR, postData and the public-values schema in StateBridge, the withdrawal root and the forced-inclusion inbox.
  3. M12 Recursion prototype Poseidon2 inner layer, keccak256 final layer, with the Fibonacci AIR as the payload.
  4. M13 Payment AIR set Hash-based signatures and the lookup layer; wallet key derivation and index persistence.
  5. M14 Escape hatch, governance, defense in depth External review of the verifier and the circuits, then a public testnet deployment of the sequencer, the prover and the submitter.
