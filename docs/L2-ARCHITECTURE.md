# L2 architecture decision: a post-quantum validity L2 on QRL 2.0

Milestone M9. This document turns the measured verifier gas (`docs/VERIFIER.md`),
the proof sizes (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`, the prover's `sizes` command) and the bridge
skeleton (`docs/BRIDGE.md`) into a design decision for a validity L2 on the 64-byte
QRL 2.0 network. Every primitive on the path stays hash based or lattice based:
the STARK (keccak256, Goldilocks), the L2 signatures (hash-based, with the circuit
hash suite pending review) and the L1 boundary (ML-DSA-87). Nothing here depends
on pairings or on discrete logarithms, which QRL 2.0 does not offer anyway.

How to read the numbers:

| Label      | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| measured   | Receipt or `qrl_estimateGas` value from the gqrl developer node at 20,000,000 gas limit, hypc `0.2.0-develop.2026.8.25`; `docs/VERIFIER.md` cells are optimizer runs 200, `docs/GAS-REPORT.md` cells are runs 1000000 (2 to 6 percent cheaper, same code-size cap) |
| exact size | Byte length of a proof the prover produced (`test/vectors/`, `test/vectors/large/`)                                                                                                                                                                                |
| model      | The calibrated gas model of section 2.5, fitted to the measured cells (deviation between -0.9 and +1.1 percent on every measured cell)                                                                                                                             |
| formula    | The prover's `sizes` command (pruned sibling count model); it overestimates exact sizes by 5 to 10 percent, so the model scales formula bytes and hashes by 0.95                                                                                                   |
| cited      | External claim with a URL in the Sources section; treat as hedged, the numbers move with upstream releases                                                                                                                                                         |

Security scope: the current implementation is a validity proof for scaling and
is not zero knowledge. All current FRI presets are experimental benchmark
profiles. Poseidon2 is an unselected benchmark candidate after the August 2026
Poseidon attack, and Rescue Prime stands beside it as the second candidate;
[`SECURITY-STATUS.md`](SECURITY-STATUS.md) is the controlling status and
release-gate document.

The verifier's costs are deterministic QRVM gas: the developer node and the Kurtosis
composition price the same opcodes, so the measured cells carry over to the public
network unchanged. `docs/GAS-REPORT.md` (generated by `npm run gas:report`) carries
the developer-node receipts of 35 cells at n = 10 to 20 (runs 1000000) and the nine
large vectors the node's transaction pool refused as `oversized data`; the Kurtosis
run of milestone M7 adds the release-gate receipts and changes no number that
matters here.

## 1. Executive summary and recommendation

Recommendation: build a validity rollup whose data availability lives in QRL
calldata, whose final on-chain proof uses the Plonky3 Goldilocks/keccak256 shape
that `StarkVerifier` already measures, whose batch proofs are aggregated by
two-layer recursion (a reviewed circuit hash inside, keccak256 at the last layer),
whose L2 accounts use a versioned hash-based signature scheme, and whose L1
boundary keeps ML-DSA-87 through the bridge. Preset c3 is the current cost model;
the production FRI profile comes from the soundness gate. Start with a centralized
sequencer and a single prover with forced inclusion and an escape hatch on L1;
move to a proof market and then to sequencer decentralization. Build a custom AIR
set for payments first and treat a RISC-V zkVM as a later programmability step.

The numbers that decide this:

- One c3 verification costs 2.10M gas at a 2^12 trace (measured, direct call, 43,152
  proof bytes, runs 200) and 4.14M gas at 2^20 (measured, direct call, 105,873
  bytes, runs 1000000; 4.32M through the gas meter; 4.30M by the runs-200 model).
  Both sit far below the 8M target and the 20M block cap. Calldata is 22 to 34
  percent of the small cells and 39 to 41 percent at 2^20 because Merkle siblings
  grow with the tree height.
- The binding limit is the transaction size cap of the node's transaction pool
  (128 KB, the same constant as go-ethereum), reached by c3 near a 2^22 trace, by c2
  at 2^20 (134,801 proof bytes, a 142,491-byte transaction, refused) and by c1
  already at 2^16 (147,462 bytes, refused). Gas would allow c3 up to 2^28 (7.1M,
  model). Proof size therefore caps a single on-chain proof at about 2^20 to 2^22
  rows, and recursion or staged verification is the way past it.
- A transfer needs about 16 bytes of calldata when the signature stays off chain
  (256 gas at the non-zero byte rate). With the proof and two 123 KB data
  transactions a batch of 15,400 transfers costs 8.4M gas, 540 gas per transfer, 39
  times below the 21,000 gas of a native transfer that also drags a 7.2 KB
  ML-DSA-87 envelope through every block. With recursion the per-transfer cost
  approaches the calldata floor of about 260 gas.
- A 2^20 trace holds roughly 1,000 to 3,500 hash-based-signature transfers, so
  without recursion the verification cost dominates (1.5k to 4.2k gas per
  transfer). Recursion is what turns the verifier's fixed 4.3M into an amortized
  cost, which is why it is on the critical path.
- An off-chain data committee attested with ML-DSA-87 costs about 245k gas per
  committee signature on L1 (precompile plus 7.2 KB of calldata), so a 5-of-7
  attestation costs as much as 76 KB of calldata. A validium only beats the rollup
  on gas above roughly 4,800 transfers per batch, and it does so by adding a trust
  assumption a post-quantum-branded chain should avoid.
- ML-DSA-87 verification inside a proof is one to two orders of magnitude more
  expensive than the current Poseidon2 hash-signature cost model (about 300 Keccak-f permutations
  and 24 NTTs per verification, about 3M RISC-V instructions in a zkVM, cited), so
  L2 accounts get a STARK-native scheme and ML-DSA-87 stays at the bridge.

### 2.3 Proof sizes (exact) and their structure

A proof is a 422 to 668 byte transcript prefix plus, per committed matrix and per
FRI round, the opened rows or sibling values of every query and the pruned Merkle
siblings (`docs/PROTOCOL.md` section 11). The sibling count of a block with `Q`
queries into a tree of height `h` is about `Q * (h - log2 Q) + min(Q, 2^h)`, which
is why bytes grow linearly in `n` at fixed `Q`, and why c1 (`Q = 100`) is the
largest at every size:

| Preset | n = 10 | n = 12 |  n = 16 |  n = 20 |
| ------ | -----: | -----: | ------: | ------: |
| c1     | 53,904 | 81,744 | 143,814 | 226,385 |
| c2     | 36,016 | 49,968 |  88,838 | 136,529 |
| c3     | 31,600 | 43,152 |  69,510 | 103,217 |

(`c3-binary` at n = 16: 127,614 bytes.) The `n = 16` and `n = 20` vectors were
produced by the prover during this milestone and live in the untracked
`test/vectors/large/`; the same numbers come out of
`cargo run --release --manifest-path prover/Cargo.toml -- sizes --preset c3 --log-n 20 --vector <file>`.

### 2.4 Soundness of the presets

The `conjectured bits` column of the preset table is `log_blowup * queries +
query_pow_bits`: 116 for c1, 116 for c2, 118 for c3. That is the ethSTARK
conjecture (one bit of soundness per query per bit of rate), which the Plonky3
`p3-fri` crate reports the same way (cited: ethSTARK, ePrint 2021/582). The proven
bound is lower: in the Johnson-bound regime of the proximity-gaps theorem (cited:
ePrint 2020/654) a query contributes roughly `log_blowup / 2` bits before the
extension-field and list-size terms, so the presets sit near 66 to 70 provable bits
with the 16 PoW bits included. Two 2025 results falsify the up-to-capacity
proximity-gaps conjectures for Reed-Solomon codes over prime fields in some
parameter regimes (cited: ePrint 2025/2046 and 2025/2055); Goldilocks is a prime
field, so the 118-bit figure must be read as a conjecture under active revision.

Levers, priced with the model of section 2.5 at `n = 20` (runs 200; the c3 row was
measured at 4.14M at runs 1000000):

| Variant                 | Conjectured bits | Rough provable bits | Proof bytes (formula x 0.95) | Gas (model) |
| ----------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------: | ---------------------------: | ----------: |
| c3 (lb 3, Q 34, PoW 16) |              118 |                  67 |              105,873 (exact) |       4.30M |
| c3 with 24 PoW bits     |              126 |                  75 |                      105,873 |       4.30M |
| lb 4, Q 26, PoW 16      |              120 |                  68 |                       93,800 |       3.61M |
| lb 4, Q 52, PoW 16      |              224 |                 120 |                      174,421 |       6.78M |
| c3 with Q 68            |              220 |                 118 |                      196,795 |       8.10M |

Proof-of-work bits are free for the verifier (one extra keccak flush) and cost the
prover `2^bits` hashes, so raising the query PoW to 24 bits is the cheapest
conjectured-bits lever. A provable-regime deployment (`lb 4, Q 52`) fits the gas
budget and misses the 128 KB transaction cap (section 4.2); the blowup increase
doubles the prover's LDE work.

### 2.5 Extrapolation to n = 16, 20, 24, 28 (model)

The model reads the per-phase costs above and reproduces every measured cell:

```
total      = 21,000 + 16 * bytes + phases
phases     = prepare + 12,400 + friTranscript + merkle + reducedOpenings + foldChains
prepare    = 54,000 + 50 * bytes / 64
friTranscript = 10,900 + 1,500 * rounds + 100 * Q
merkle     = 150 * Q * log2(Q) + 315 * hashes            (hashes = unique leaves + frontier compressions of every block)
reducedOpenings = 3,340 * Q
foldChains = Q * (sum over rounds of fold(arity) + 2,400)  with fold(2) = 2,766, fold(4) = 3,913, fold(8) = 6,324, fold(16) = 10,784
```

Bytes and hash counts are exact for every vector that exists (n <= 20) and formula
values scaled by 0.95 for `n = 24` and `n = 28`. Deviation from the six measured
runs-200 `estimate` cells and `c3-binary n = 12`: -0.9 to +1.1 percent. Against the
three large cells measured at runs 1000000 the runs-200 model is 3.4 to 4.0 percent
high (c3 n = 16: 3.16M against 3.05M; c3 n = 20: 4.30M against 4.14M; c2 n = 16:

## 3. Rollup or validium on QRL

### 3.1 The data availability unit

Ethereum's rollup accounting puts a compressed transfer at about 12 bytes (cited:
Buterin, "An Incomplete Guide to Rollups"), and QRL's calldata price is the same
16 gas per non-zero byte and 4 per zero byte (`params/protocol_params.go` of the
node, verified), with no blob transactions and no calldata floor price of the
EIP-7623 kind (cited). The differences on QRL are the 64-byte L1 addresses and the
7,219-byte ML-DSA-87 key plus signature that every L1 transaction carries in its
envelope (`PublicKey` and `Signature` fields of the dynamic-fee transaction type,
verified in the node sources). Neither belongs in L2 calldata:

| Item                                                                                       | Bytes on L1 calldata | Gas at 16 / byte | Note                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------: | ---------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Transfer as transaction data (sender index 4, recipient index 4, amount 5, fee 2, index 1) |                  ~16 |              256 | signature (2.8 KB hash-based or 4.6 KB ML-DSA-87) stays off chain; the STARK proves it |
| Transfer as state diff (two touched accounts: index 4 + new balance 6 + nonce 2 each)      |                  ~24 |              384 | batch-level dedup of hot accounts lowers this                                          |
| New account (32-byte key commitment, once)                                                 |                  +32 |             +512 | +64 more if the L1 address is bound in the leaf                                        |
| Withdrawal request (L1 address 64, amount 8, pkHash 32)                                    |                 ~104 |           ~1,664 | rare; also hashed into the withdrawal tree (section 6)                                 |

Gas per byte is quoted at the non-zero rate, so the table is conservative:
small amounts and low indices carry zero bytes at 4 gas. Addresses are replaced by
account indices in the L2 state tree; the 64-byte L1 address appears once at
account creation (if bound) and once per withdrawal.

### 3.2 Two ways to carry the data

The node's transaction pool rejects transactions above 131,072 bytes
(`txMaxSize = 4 * 32 KB` in the legacy pool, a compile-time constant; the
validation error is `oversized data`, recorded nine times in `docs/GAS-REPORT.md`).
The measured overhead of a `verify` transaction over its proof bytes is 7,690 bytes
(the 7,219-byte ML-DSA-87 envelope plus RLP and ABI framing), so the largest proof
a single transaction can carry is 123,382 bytes. A c3 proof at n = 20 (105,873
bytes, a 113,563-byte transaction) leaves about 17.5 KB in the same transaction,
which caps inline data at about 1,090 transfers. The batch data therefore goes into
separate data transactions:

```
postData(bytes data)          // stores keccak256(data) in a per-batch list, emits DataPosted
submitBatch(prevRoot, newRoot, publicValues, proof)   // publicValues commit to the data hashes
```

The contract hashes calldata at 6 gas per 64-byte word (keccak256 on the QRVM is
30 + 6 per word), so a full 123,400-byte data transaction costs 21,000 base plus
1,974,400 calldata plus about 12,000 hashing plus 22,100 for the storage slot,
about 2.03M gas, and carries 7,710 transfers at 16 bytes. The STARK binds the
data by recomputing the same keccak256 inside the circuit (about 1,810 Keccak-f
permutations per 246 KB, section 6.2), which is the one place keccak must appear
in a circuit that otherwise uses the selected hash suite.

### 3.3 Gas budget per batch

The consensus cap is 20,000,000 per block. A steady-state target of at most half a
block per slot for the L2 (10M) leaves the other half for wallet transfers, swaps,
QNS and staking traffic; the data and proof transactions are independent, so the
block builder can also spread them over consecutive slots. With the c3 proof at
n = 20 (4.14M direct, measured at runs 1000000; 4.30M through the bridge):

| Batch composition                               |               Gas | Transfers carried | Gas per transfer | Comment                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------: | ----------------: | ---------------: | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| proof only (validium)                           |             4.30M |   (trace-limited) |                  | data attested off chain                                  |
| proof + 17.5 KB inline data                     |             4.58M |             1,090 |            4,200 | one transaction, fits the pool cap                       |
| proof + 1 data transaction                      |             6.33M |             7,710 |              821 |                                                          |
| proof + 2 data transactions                     |             8.36M |            15,420 |              542 | recommended steady state under a 10M budget              |
| proof + 3 data transactions                     |            10.39M |            23,130 |              449 | exceeds a 10M budget, fits the block                     |
| k batches per proof (recursion), 2 data tx each | 4.30M + k x 4.06M |        k x 15,420 |    263 + 279 / k | floor is the calldata cost of about 260 gas per transfer |

For comparison a native L1 transfer costs 21,000 gas, so a block holds 952 of them
and 6.9 MB of ML-DSA-87 envelopes. The rollup's per-transfer cost is 25 to 80 times
lower at the data-bound batch sizes and 5 to 14 times lower when a 2^20 trace
limits a batch to 1,000 to 3,500 transfers (4,200 down to about 1,500 gas per
transfer with a partially filled data transaction).

### 3.4 Transactions per second

Slot time decides the per-second numbers. The Kurtosis composition used for the
measurements runs 6-second slots with 8 slots per epoch
(`config/kurtosis-stark.yaml`). The Qrysm fork's mainnet parameter set specifies
60-second slots and 128 slots per epoch (`config/params/mainnet_config.go` of the
fork, verified), and the public testnet is expected to follow that set; confirm
against the live beacon node's spec endpoint before sizing a product. One batch
per slot:

| Limit                                               | Transfers per batch | TPS at 6 s slots | TPS at 60 s slots |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------: | ---------------: | ----------------: |
| 2^20 trace, hash-based signatures, no recursion     |      1,000 to 3,500 |       170 to 580 |          17 to 58 |
| data bound, 2 data transactions per slot (8.4M gas) |              15,420 |            2,570 |               257 |
| data bound, full block of data (about 9 data tx)    |             ~70,000 |          ~11,600 |            ~1,160 |
| native L1 transfers (20M / 21,000)                  |                 952 |              159 |                16 |

Finality follows the same parameter set: about 96 seconds in the Kurtosis
composition (two epochs of 48 seconds) and about 4.3 hours with 60-second slots and
128-slot epochs. Withdrawals should wait for L1 finality of the batch that contains
them; soft confirmations from the sequencer cover the user experience in between.

### 3.5 Validium with a data committee

A validium posts only the proof and has a committee attest that the batch data is
available. On QRL the attestation is expensive because every committee signature
is ML-DSA-87: 125,000 gas for the precompile plus 7,219 bytes of key and signature
in calldata (about 115,500 gas) plus framing, about 245k gas per signature; a
5-of-7 threshold verified on chain is about 1.22M gas per batch, the price of
76 KB of calldata (4,760 transfers). An alternative has the committee sign with
a hash-based scheme and the STARK verify the attestation in circuit, which
removes the L1 cost and makes the committee part of the proven statement.

Trust assumptions of the validium: the committee can withhold data, which freezes
withdrawals unless an escape hatch with full state exists (it cannot exist without
the data), and a colluding committee plus sequencer can hide the state needed to
exit. The rollup's only assumption beyond the hash functions is that QRL itself
stays live and censorship-resistant, which the escape hatch and forced inclusion
of section 6 turn into user-exercisable rights.

Recommendation for a post-quantum-branded chain: rollup. Its story is "every
byte needed to reconstruct the L2 is on QRL, and every proof is hash based";
a validium replaces that with a committee. A volition (per-account choice of
calldata or committee data, one state root) is a later product option once the
rollup runs; it changes the data path and nothing in the verifier.

## 4. Batch sizing and proving cadence

### 4.1 Trace size, proving time, proof size, gas

For a trace of `2^n` rows and width `w`, the prover computes low-degree extensions
of size `2^(n + log_blowup)` per column, hashes every extended row into a Merkle
tree, builds the quotient, and runs FRI; proving work is roughly linear in
`w * 2^(n + log_blowup)` with an `n` factor from the FFTs. The blowup is the lever
that trades prover work against verifier cost: c1 (`log_blowup 1`) costs the prover
a quarter of c3's LDE and hashing and costs the verifier 2.4 times the gas at
n = 20 (10.15M by the model against the measured 4.14M) and twice the bytes. c3 is
the right point for an L1 whose calldata is the scarce resource.

The Fibonacci prover here has width 2, so its timings say little about a real
AIR. The vector generation log of this milestone (which includes the mirror
verifier, the transcript logging and the JSON serialization of every intermediate
value) took 0.5 to 67 seconds per vector at n = 16 and n = 20 on a 20-thread
laptop; the proving inside that is a fraction, and the c3 n = 20 run is dominated
by the serialization of the 105 KB proof and its transcript. Treat those as upper
bounds on single-machine proving of a width-2 trace. The shape for a payment AIR:

- Memory: a 2^20 trace of 300 columns at blowup 8 is `2^23 * 300 * 8` bytes, about
  20 GB of extended trace before the Merkle trees; at 2^24 it is 320 GB. Single
  proofs above 2^20 to 2^22 with c3 need a large machine or a lower blowup; this
  rules out one huge proof and pushes the design towards many 2^18 to 2^20
  batch proofs plus recursion.
- Time: multi-threaded Plonky3 provers on a workstation prove keccak-heavy 2^20
  traces in seconds to tens of seconds (cited, Polygon's Plonky3 benchmarks; the
  figures move with hardware and release); expect minutes per batch proof of a
  width-300 AIR on CPU, single-threaded runs one to two orders of magnitude
  slower. GPU provers of the SP1 and OpenVM kind are the throughput path if a
  zkVM is adopted (section 8).
- Proof size and gas follow the tables of section 2: at fixed preset they grow
  linearly in `n`, and every extra committed matrix (a second AIR, a permutation
  argument) adds one Merkle block per query, about 15 to 20 KB and 0.4 to 0.5M gas
  at n = 20.

### 4.2 Where a single proof stops fitting

| Ceiling                                                 | c3                                                                                       | c2                                                     | c1                                                     | Source                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Transaction pool size cap (123,382 usable proof bytes)  | n = 20 fits (113,563-byte transaction, 17.5 KB spare); n = 22 borderline; n = 24 exceeds | n = 16 fits; n = 20 refused (142,491-byte transaction) | n = 12 fits; n = 16 refused (155,163-byte transaction) | node `txMaxSize` constant; `docs/GAS-REPORT.md` rejections |
| 8M gas target per verification                          | n <= 28                                                                                  | n <= 24                                                | n <= 16                                                | model                                                      |
| 20M block cap                                           | beyond n = 28                                                                            | beyond n = 28                                          | n <= 28                                                | model                                                      |
| Verifier memory (about 190 KB peak measured at Q = 100) | no limit in range                                                                        | no limit in range                                      | no limit in range                                      | `docs/VERIFIER.md` section 2                               |

The size cap is a node default, identical to go-ethereum's, and every block
producer applies it in its own pool, so raising it is effectively a network
parameter change even though no consensus rule mentions it. Three ways past it, in
order of preference:

1. Recursion (section 4.3): the final proof stays at n = 20 or below regardless
   of how much work it attests.
2. Staged verification (`docs/STAGED-VERIFICATION.md`): split the proof by block
   across several transactions keyed by `proofId`; every block is addressable from
   the prefix by design of the layout. Cost: one 21,000 base per transaction plus
   about 22k per stored fact plus the replayed prefix; a c3 n = 24 proof (about
   145 KB) would need two transactions and about 0.1M extra gas.
3. Raising `txMaxSize` in the node: only if the network operators agree; the
   design should not depend on it.

### 4.3 Recursion: the two-layer candidate design

Verifying a keccak-committed STARK inside another STARK is expensive because
keccak256 is expensive in arithmetic circuits: a Keccak-f[1600] permutation costs
24 rows of an AIR with about 2,600 columns in Plonky3's keccak AIR (cited, Plonky3
repository), and a c3 n = 20 proof needs 3,135 hashes plus the transcript flushes,
about 200M trace cells just for the hashing. Two candidates sit under the review
gate of `SECURITY-STATUS.md`, and neither is selected. Poseidon2 (cited, ePrint
2023/323) is the cost-study candidate: over Goldilocks it costs one row of a few
hundred columns per permutation, about 200 times less than keccak. Rescue Prime
(cited, ePrint 2019/426 and 2020/1143; `p3-rescue` in the pinned Plonky3, with a
Rescue Prime Optimized Goldilocks instance, cited, ePrint 2022/1577) is the
conservative candidate that a QRL core contributor recommended for Merkle
commitments because of its margin against algebraic attacks: every round applies
`x^7` and then `x^(1/7)` to the full state, so there is no partial layer, and the
round count carries the specification's 50 percent margin. Its proving cost is
higher. The AIR constrains the inverse S-box through `y^7 = x` at the same degree
as the forward one, so the constraint count per permutation stays within a small
factor of Poseidon2's; the native evaluation of `x^(1/7)` is a full exponentiation
per lane, so trace generation is several times slower (estimate; the M10 benchmark
measures both). On the QRVM the ratio is reversed: a keccak compression costs 36
gas, the candidate Poseidon2 permutation would cost tens of thousands of gas
(roughly 340 S-box multiplications and 30 linear layers over 512-bit words,
estimate: 35k to 40k gas per permutation), and a Rescue Prime permutation about
two to three times that (one modexp call of about 440 gas per inverse S-box and a
dense 12 x 12 matrix product twice per round, estimate) until the Rescue Prime
precompile the QRL side plans at slot 0x20 exists. This motivates a hash-agile
two-layer shape:

| Layer            | Field / hash                                | Verified by                                   | Proof shape                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Batch proofs     | Goldilocks, selected circuit hash           | the aggregation AIR (in circuit)              | any size; stay off L1                                                         |
| Aggregation tree | Goldilocks, selected circuit hash           | the next aggregation layer or the final layer | k proofs in, 1 out; repeat until one proof covers the desired span of batches |
| Final proof      | Goldilocks, keccak256 Merkle and challenger | `StarkVerifier` on QRL                        | one AIR whose statement verifies one aggregation proof; c3, n <= 20           |

What the on-chain verifier reuses unchanged: `Goldilocks.hyp`, `Fp2.hyp`,
`KeccakChallenger.hyp`, `MerkleMultiProof.hyp`, `FriVerifier.hyp` and the transcript
flow of `StarkVerifierCore`. What changes:

- `ProofLayout.hyp`: a wider trace (hundreds of columns where the Fibonacci AIR
  has 2, so the opened rows and the leaf preimages grow), several quotient
  chunks (the
  aggregation AIR's constraint degree is above 2, so the quotient is committed in
  `2^ceil(log2(degree - 1))` chunks) and, if the AIR uses lookups, one more
  commitment round with its own challenges before the quotient. The layout carries
  a version byte for this.
- `air/FibonacciAir.hyp` is replaced by the aggregation AIR's constraint
  evaluator at `zeta`: pure polynomial evaluation of the selected hash round function
  and the FRI-fold algebra at one point, so nothing is hashed on chain. Its cost scales with
  the number of constraint terms (about 100 gas per extension multiplication):
  estimate 0.5 to 1.5M gas.
- The opened values of a width-`w` trace add `2 * w * 16` bytes of calldata and
  their observation into the transcript: about 16 KB and 0.3M gas at `w = 500`.

So the final proof's on-chain cost is the c3 n = 20 cell plus roughly 1 to 2M:
about 5.3 to 6.3M gas (estimate) per aggregated proof, independent of how many
batches it covers. The Plonky3 recursion library currently uses Poseidon2-based
hashing and supports chaining layers and aggregating independent proofs (cited,
Plonky3-recursion book). Adopting it therefore depends on the hash review as well
as confirming a Goldilocks configuration and keccak-committed outer layer against
the selected release. A Rescue Prime inner layer needs its own hash AIR in that
recursion stack, which the M10 benchmark prices together with the permutation
itself.

### 4.4 Cadence

Without recursion: one batch proof per slot at most, batch size bounded by the
trace (about 1,000 to 3,500 transfers at 2^20), proving must finish within the
slot time or batches queue up; per-transfer cost 1.5k to 4.2k gas.

With recursion: the sequencer closes a batch every slot (or every few seconds
under load), the prover produces batch proofs continuously on a pool of machines,
the aggregator folds them, and the submitter posts one final proof every `m`
slots together with the data transactions of the covered batches. The data
transactions can be posted as soon as each batch closes, so data availability
leads the proof by minutes; the state root advances when the proof lands. A
reasonable first target: data every slot, proof every 10 slots (one minute at 6 s
slots, ten minutes at 60 s), 4.3 to 6.3M gas per proof, 540 gas per transfer at
15,000 transfers per batch and about 290 gas per transfer at ten batches per proof.

## 5. L2 signature scheme

### 5.1 ML-DSA-87 verified in circuit

ML-DSA-87 (FIPS 204, cited) has `k = 8`, `l = 7`, a 2,592-byte public key and a
4,627-byte signature over `q = 8,380,417` (23 bits). A verification expands the
`8 x 7` matrix `A` from the 32-byte seed with SHAKE128 (about 280 Keccak-f
permutations, five per polynomial), hashes the public key and the message with
SHAKE256 (about 30 permutations), samples the challenge polynomial, computes
`NTT(z)` (7 polynomials), `NTT(c)` (1), `NTT(t1 * 2^d)` (8) and eight inverse NTTs
(24 transforms of 256 points, about 24,600 butterflies), 64 pointwise products of
256 coefficients, the hint decoding, `UseHint`, the `w1` encoding hash and the
infinity-norm check on `z`. In a Goldilocks AIR the `q`-arithmetic is emulated:
products of 23-bit values fit below `2^46`, so each butterfly is one field
multiplication plus a range-checked reduction, cheap; the SHAKE expansions are the
cost, 310 permutations at 24 rows and about 2,600 columns each, about 20M trace
cells per signature, plus about 1M cells for the NTTs and the decoding.

In a RISC-V zkVM the same verification is about 3.09M instructions for ML-DSA-87
(1.16M for ML-DSA-44, 776k after NTT optimization) in a scalar/vector RV64 build,
with a Keccak-f permutation at 4,000 or more scalar instructions (cited, Saarinen,
RISC-V Summit 2023). Without a Keccak precompile that is about 3M cycles per
signature; with one, roughly half. A published SP1 Dilithium verifier produces
Groth16 wrappers in about 22 seconds on Succinct's prover network (cited,
Dilithium ZK), which is a single-signature figure; a batch of 10,000 transfers
would be 30G cycles and hours of GPU time at the throughputs zkVM vendors publish.
Per transaction this is the wrong order of magnitude for a payment L2 today; it
becomes viable when a Keccak-accelerated zkVM or a dedicated lattice AIR brings
the cost near 1M cells per signature.

### 5.2 STARK-native hash-based signatures

WOTS+ one-time signatures (cited, Hülsing 2013) organized in an XMSS tree (cited,
RFC 8391) need only a hash function. Under the current Poseidon2 cost model over
Goldilocks, verification is a few hundred to about a thousand permutations at one
AIR row of a few hundred columns each, about 0.1 to 0.3M trace cells, one to two
orders of magnitude below ML-DSA-87. The estimate must be rerun for the selected
hash suite; with Rescue Prime the permutation count is unchanged and each
permutation costs more (section 4.3). Ethereum's post-quantum consensus work has
formalized exactly this family: "Hash-Based Multi-Signatures for Post-Quantum Ethereum"
(cited, ePrint 2025/055) gives XMSS-style schemes with SHA-3 or Poseidon2
instantiations and parameter tables at a stated security level, and LeanSig
(cited, ePrint 2025/1332) adds an incomparable encoding that lowers verification
hashing for a given signature size. Those parameter tables are the starting
point; the L2 takes only the single-signature verification cost and the key
lifetime from them and leaves the aggregation features unused.

Properties that matter for an L2 account scheme:

- Signature size: about 2 to 4 KB with 32-byte nodes (67 chains for a 256-bit
  digest at `w = 16`, plus an authentication path of `h` nodes). These bytes reach
  the sequencer and the prover only; nothing hash-based is ever posted on L1.
- Statefulness: each one-time key must be used once. In an L2 this hazard becomes
  a state rule: the account leaf stores the next allowed leaf index, the STARK
  enforces that every signature uses that index and increments it, and a second
  use of an index is rejected like a nonce replay. A wallet restored on two devices
  can still sign two different messages with the same index, but only one enters
  the state and the other signature is useless for a forgery on the L2 because
  that index is spent. Wallets must persist the index; derive the key tree from
  the wallet seed so that a backup of the seed remains sufficient.
- Key lifetime: `2^h` transactions per key (`h = 20` gives a million); rotation
  through account abstraction (below) before exhaustion, or a two-level tree
  (XMSS^MT) for more.
- Circuit cost: one reviewed hash suite shared by signatures, the state tree and
  recursion, so one AIR serves everything.

### 5.3 Account abstraction with key rotation

L2 accounts carry `(scheme_id, key_commitment, next_index)`; the leaf names its
scheme and commits to its key.
The state transition dispatches on `scheme_id`; rotation is an L2 transaction
signed by the current key that installs a new commitment (a new hash-based tree,
a different parameter set, or later an ML-DSA-87 key once that is affordable).
First registration happens either in an L1 deposit, whose leaf can bind the
depositor's L1 address and the L2 key commitment, or in an L2 transaction signed
by an existing key. Withdrawals keep the ML-DSA-87 boundary of `docs/BRIDGE.md`:
the L2 withdrawal request names the L1 recipient and the `pkHash` that must sign
`withdraw` on L1, and the STARK proves the request was authorized by the L2 key.
Nothing on L1 ever verifies a hash-based signature, and nothing on L2 ever
verifies ML-DSA-87.

### 5.4 Recommendation and migration path

Recommendation: versioned hash-based signatures for L2 accounts, ML-DSA-87 at the
bridge, and account abstraction from day one so the hash suite can change without
a state migration. Poseidon2 and Rescue Prime remain the benchmark candidates pending
the review gate.

1. Phase 1 (testnet): after the hash gate closes, one WOTS+/XMSS scheme with an
   explicit hash-suite identifier and reviewed parameters, keys derived from the
   wallet seed, index tracking in the wallet and enforced by the state,
   registration by deposit.
2. Phase 2: rotation transactions, a second parameter set for high-volume
   accounts (larger `h`, target-sum encoding), sequencer-side rejection of index
   reuse before it reaches the prover.
3. Phase 3 (optional): an ML-DSA-87 verification AIR or zkVM precompile as
   `scheme_id = 2`, so existing wallet keys can sign L2 transactions directly. Only
   when the cost falls near 1M cells per signature.

## 6. Bridge and trust assumptions

### 6.1 What `StateBridge` proves today

From `docs/BRIDGE.md`: a batch advances `stateRoot` only if `StarkFactRegistry`
holds (or registers in the same transaction) a fact keyed by
`(verifier, programId, keccak256(publicValues))`, where the public values encode
`(prevRoot, newRoot)` as 16 Goldilocks limbs; deposits append to a keccak256
accumulator and emit events; a withdrawal pays out against a keccak256 Merkle
inclusion proof under the current `stateRoot` plus an ML-DSA-87 signature over a
SHAKE256 digest with a domain-separating context, checked by the `0x03` and `0x06`
precompiles; facts are keyed by public values because proof bytes are malleable.
Measured: `submitBatch` 155,618 gas (mock verifier, registration included),
`deposit` 68,800, `withdraw` 345,791 (dominated by the 125,000-gas precompile and
the 7.3 KB of signature and key calldata).

the mirror and JS references and by mutation testing, and later by an external
audit), and QRL's own liveness.

## 7. Sequencer and prover topology

Data flow:

```
wallet (L2 key) --> sequencer RPC --> L2 mempool --> ordering, state transition, soft confirmation (< 1 s)
                                        |
                                        v
                               batch closed every slot
                                        |
            +---------------------------+-------------------------------+
            v                                                           v
   submitter: postData(bytes)                                  prover pool: batch proofs (selected hash suite)
   one or more data transactions per batch                              |
                                                                        v
                                                              aggregator: k proofs -> 1 (selected hash suite)
                                                                        |
                                                                        v
                                                              final layer: keccak proof (c3, n <= 20)
                                                                        |
                                                                        v
                                     submitter: submitBatch(prevRoot, newRoot, publicValues, proof)
                                                                        |
                                                                        v

```

Latency budget:

| Step                               | Kurtosis parameters (6 s slots) | Fork mainnet parameters (60 s slots) | Note                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Soft confirmation by the sequencer | < 1 s                           | < 1 s                                | trust in the sequencer until the proof lands       |
| Batch close                        | every slot                      | every slot (or every 6 s under load) |                                                    |

| Batch proof                        | tens of seconds to minutes      | same                                 | width and blowup dependent (section 4.1)           |
| Aggregation and final proof        | minutes                         | minutes                              | one final proof per 10 batches as the first target |
| Proof inclusion                    | 1 slot                          | 1 slot                               |                                                    |

Who runs what. Phase 1 (public testnet): the QuantaStark maintainers run one
sequencer, one prover pool and one submitter; the explorer indexes L2 blocks
through the sequencer RPC; the web, desktop and mobile wallets add the L2 key
derivation and the sequencer endpoint next to the existing L1 RPC proxy; the dApp
connect SDK gains an L2 signing method so the swap and naming products can settle
on the L2. Phase 2: separate prover operators paid from L2 fees (a proof market:
the sequencer publishes batch commitments, any prover submits a batch proof, the
aggregator takes the first valid one), with the sequencer still centralized.
Phase 3: sequencer decentralization or based sequencing (L1 block producers order
L2 batches), which the forced-inclusion inbox already anticipates. Whether and
under which entity a production network with user funds is operated is a
governance decision outside this document; the testnet needs none of it.

Hardware: one multi-core machine with 64 GB or more handles 2^18 to 2^20 batch
proofs of a width-300 AIR at c3 in minutes (estimate); the aggregation layer is
cheaper per proof than the batch layer because its AIR is fixed; the submitter
needs an L1 account funded for 4.3 to 6.3M gas per proof plus about 2M per data
transaction.

## 8. Path to a zkVM

SP1 Turbo used the BabyBear field, Poseidon2 hashing and Plonky3's FRI-based
STARKs, with conjectured 100-bit security under proximity-gap conjectures; SP1
Hypercube moved to the KoalaBear field and a multilinear "jagged" polynomial
commitment whose security rests on proven proximity-gap theorems (cited, SP1
security model). SP1's proof types are core (size proportional to execution), compressed
(constant-size STARK, recursively verifiable inside SP1), and Groth16 or PLONK
wrappers over BN254 for the EVM, about 260 bytes at about 270k gas and 868 bytes at
about 300k gas respectively (cited, SP1 proof types). OpenVM uses BabyBear with a
quartic extension, Poseidon2, Plonky3 STARKs with continuations and inner STARK
aggregation, and a Halo2 outer layer for EVM verification (cited, OpenVM
documentation and whitepaper).

What matches this repository: the transcript, MMCS and FRI code here verifies any
Plonky3 `TwoAdicFriPcs` proof whose configuration is Goldilocks plus keccak256
Merkle trees plus the keccak `HashChallenger`; the field, the hash and the
challenger are the whole configuration. Nothing in a zkVM's pipeline is
configured that way today: their inner layers use Poseidon2 over BabyBear or
KoalaBear (the right choice inside a circuit), and their outer layers are
pairing-based SNARKs that QRL cannot verify. So adopting a zkVM means:

1. A keccak-committed final STARK layer in the zkVM's recursion stack, replacing
   the Groth16/PLONK/Halo2 wrap: the same "verify the last recursion proof inside
   a differently committed STARK" step they already perform, with the target
   swapped. Field choice for that layer: their field (BabyBear, degree-4
   extension), which needs a second field module in Hyperion (31-bit elements in
   4 bytes, extension multiplication roughly twice the Fp2 cost, FRI folds about
   twice as expensive, rows half the bytes: gas of the same order as c3), or
   Goldilocks, which needs a BabyBear-verifying AIR over Goldilocks that nobody
   ships.
2. The recursion AIR's constraints in Hyperion (section 4.3), hundreds of columns
   and thousands of terms: estimate 1 to 3M gas on top of the c3 cell.
3. Parameter alignment: their compressed proofs target about 100 conjectured bits
   over a 31-bit field; the final keccak layer would be tuned like c3 (blowup 8,
   34 queries, 16 PoW bits) to fit the 128 KB transaction cap.
4. Risks: a fast-moving upstream (SP1 changed its proof system in 2025), the
   audit surface of a full RISC-V machine, GPU infrastructure, and the fact that
   Keccak precompiles inside zkVMs are what makes the data binding and ML-DSA-87
   affordable there, so the choice of zkVM is also a choice of precompile set.

The alternative is a custom AIR set for a payment L2 on Plonky3 directly: a
selected-hash AIR (state tree, signatures, recursion), a keccak AIR (data binding,
deposit accumulator, withdrawal root), an account-update AIR (balances, nonces,
indices, fees) and a range-check AIR, connected by a lookup argument. Plonky3's
`uni-stark` proves one AIR; multi-AIR proofs with lookups need the machine layer
that SP1 and OpenVM built on top of Plonky3 or a comparable in-house layer
(hedge: check what the current Plonky3 release ships). On the verifier side this
adds mixed-height or multiple commitments per round (the `MerkleMultiProof`
library assumes one height per commitment today), one permutation commitment
round with its challenges, and one constraint evaluator per AIR: about 0.4 to
0.5M gas and 15 to 20 KB per extra commitment at n = 20, so a four-AIR machine
lands near 6 to 7M gas per final proof (estimate). Costs: cryptographic design and
audit of a bespoke machine, and the absence of general programmability.
Benefits: every cell of
the trace does payment work, the recursion stays in Goldilocks and reuses the
verifier as is, and the proof shape stays within what this repository verifies.

Decision: custom AIR set first. Layout version 2 and the hash-suite boundary come
before a concrete hash AIR. Keep the zkVM path open by keeping the layout versioned
and the field module swappable; revisit when a zkVM ships a keccak-committed final
layer or when the L2 needs general contracts.

## 9. Open questions and next experiments

1. Hash security gate: resolve whether the 2026 S-box-skipping result applies to
   the exact Poseidon2 Goldilocks candidates, fix the Rescue Prime instance and
   confirm its round count and constant derivation, recompute parameters for
   both, and compare a conservative Keccak baseline as specified in
   `SECURITY-STATUS.md`; the Least Authority audit's notes on Poseidon2 round
   constants and MDS checks (`VERIFIER.md` section 8) are inputs.
2. Layout version 2 and a hash-suite interface: add multi-commitment,
   mixed-height and quotient-chunk support, then benchmark Poseidon2 and Rescue
   Prime only under experimental suite identifiers. Target below +1M gas at
   n = 20.
3. Keccak AIR proof at 2^16 rows to size the data-binding and deposit-chain
   cost per batch (about 24 rows per 136 bytes of data, section 3.2).
4. Full protocol gate: build `gqrl` from the clean pinned source, require the
   provenance check, and run the scheduled compiler, Rust, Node and live QRVM
   suites with an attached evidence record.
5. Staged verification: implement and measure only if a 2^24 single proof turns
   out to be needed before recursion exists.
6. Precompile QIP: the Merkle walks (39 percent of compute at n = 20) run at 315
   gas per hash with a 36-gas keccak inside, and the folds (50 percent) at about
   900 gas per pair; a "verify pruned keccak multi-opening" precompile at about 40
   gas per node would save about 0.85M and a Goldilocks fold precompile about
   1.1M per c3 n = 20 verification. Both need a network upgrade; write the note
   after the L2's AIR set is fixed so the precompile matches real proofs. The QRL
   side plans an extended precompile set with Rescue Prime at slot 0x20 and a
   STARK verifier at slot 0x21; the note takes those slots as given and states
   what a keccak-committed final layer needs from a 0x21 verifier.
7. Calldata compression: measure state-diff sizes on a simulated workload;
   structural compression only (index addressing, variable-length amounts, zero
   bytes at 4 gas), since nothing on L1 can decompress a general-purpose codec.
8. Withdrawal aggregation: `withdraw` costs 346k gas because of the ML-DSA-87
   call and its 7.3 KB of calldata; a batch-level "proven withdrawals" list that
   pays out without a per-user signature (authorization proven in circuit) cuts
   that to a transfer plus an inclusion proof, about 60k.
9. Hash-based parameters: after suite selection, pick a parameter set, count
   permutations per verification in the AIR, and fix the wallet's index
   persistence and seed derivation.
10. Recursion prototype: an experimental selected-hash c3 proof of the Fibonacci AIR
    verified inside a Goldilocks AIR, then that proof re-committed with keccak and
    verified by `StarkVerifier`; the first end-to-end test of the two-layer design.
11. Soundness policy: derive a production profile from current proven analysis
    and the chosen target. Evaluate the provable-regime `lb 4, Q 52` candidate
    (6.8M gas and 174 KB) with staging or recursion; keep c3 as a benchmark.
12. Hyperion compiler: the legacy code generator breaks `mldsa87verify` argument
    order and public mapping getters (`docs/compiler/HYPC-LEGACY-CODEGEN-DEFECTS.md`);
    every bridge contract must be built with `--via-ir` until the upstream fixes
    land, and the fixes should be upstreamed before any external team builds on
    the bridge.
13. Network parameters: confirm slot time, epoch length and the pool size cap of
